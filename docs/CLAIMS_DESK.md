# FlightClaimly Claims Desk

Last Updated: 2026-08-24

## Purpose

The Claims Desk is FlightClaimly's operational framework for investigating, evaluating, pursuing and resolving individual passenger claims.

It is separate from Product / Growth and the public Knowledge Engine.

The Claims Desk exists to answer one central question:

Can FlightClaimly responsibly and successfully recover compensation or reimbursement for this passenger?

Every case should be evidence-led, legally grounded and commercially sensible.

---

# Core Principles

1. Evidence before conclusions.
2. Reconstruct the journey before determining eligibility.
3. Separate different legal heads of claim.
4. Verify jurisdiction and limitation before making promises.
5. Prefer primary legal and official sources.
6. Never overstate uncertain law or facts.
7. Keep customer communication understandable.
8. Keep airline demands precise and legally structured.
9. Never promise compensation before liability is sufficiently established.
10. No Win, No Fee does not mean every claim should be pursued.
11. Customer authority must be valid before FlightClaimly formally represents the passenger.
12. Additional litigation costs require separate customer approval.
13. Every adult passenger requires individual authority where applicable.
14. Preserve evidence and reasoning throughout the claim lifecycle.
15. Learn from resolved claims without exposing customer-specific information.

# 1. Intake

Establish:

- passenger name
- all additional passengers
- passenger ages where relevant
- contact information
- airline
- flight number(s)
- travel date
- booking reference
- origin
- destination
- connections
- ticket / booking structure
- disruption type
- customer's description of events
- previous correspondence with airline
- previous compensation or reimbursement
- previous complaints or legal action

Do not assume the customer's legal characterization is correct.

Record the facts first.

---

# 2. Evidence Review

Potential evidence includes:

- booking confirmation
- e-ticket
- boarding pass
- airline emails
- cancellation notices
- delay notifications
- rebooking information
- receipts
- bank/card evidence
- hotel invoices
- transportation receipts
- photographs
- screenshots
- previous airline correspondence
- authority correspondence
- ADR decisions
- regulator correspondence

Determine what each document actually proves.

Distinguish:

- confirmed fact
- customer statement
- third-party statement
- inference
- unresolved fact

Missing evidence should be identified before airline submission where material.

---

# 3. Itinerary Reconstruction

Reconstruct the complete contracted journey.

For every segment establish:

- operating carrier
- marketing carrier where relevant
- flight number
- scheduled departure
- scheduled arrival
- actual departure
- actual arrival
- origin
- destination
- connection airport
- booking relationship
- replacement flight where applicable

For connecting journeys determine whether segments formed one reservation / single booking.

The final destination is critical for EU261 delay analysis.

Do not analyze isolated flight segments before understanding the complete itinerary.

---

# 4. Disruption Reconstruction

Determine exactly what happened.

Possible disruption categories:

- cancellation
- long delay
- missed connection
- denied boarding
- downgrade
- rerouting
- schedule change
- baggage-related issue
- combination of disruptions

Establish chronology.

Where possible determine:

- initial disruption
- airline explanation
- resulting missed connections
- rerouting offered
- rerouting accepted
- final arrival delay
- passenger expenses
- assistance provided by airline

---

# 5. Applicable Legal Framework

Determine which legal regime applies before calculating compensation.

Potential frameworks include:

- Regulation (EC) No 261/2004
- UK261
- Montreal Convention
- national contract law
- national consumer law
- applicable ADR rules
- national enforcement procedures

For EU261 establish:

- departure jurisdiction
- arrival jurisdiction
- operating carrier status
- applicability to the complete itinerary
- relevant CJEU case law where necessary

Do not assume that every loss caused by a flight disruption belongs under EU261 Article 7.

---

# 6. Separate Heads of Claim

Claims should be separated by legal basis.

## EU261 Article 7

Standardised compensation.

Potential amounts:

- €250
- €400
- €600

Determine distance and applicable reduction rules.

## EU261 Article 8

Reimbursement or rerouting rights.

## EU261 Article 9

Right to care.

Potential recoverable expenses may include:

- reasonable meals
- refreshments
- hotel accommodation
- transport between airport and accommodation
- necessary communications

Receipts should be assessed individually.

## Montreal Convention / Individual Damages

Where applicable, analyze separately from standardised EU261 compensation.

Do not unnecessarily merge Article 7 compensation with individualized damages.

Different legal rules, evidentiary requirements and limitation periods may apply.

---

# 7. Extraordinary Circumstances

For EU261 compensation determine whether the airline can establish extraordinary circumstances.

Possible issues include:

- severe weather
- air traffic control restrictions
- airport closure
- security events
- external strikes
- bird strikes
- hidden manufacturing defects
- political instability

Issues normally inherent in airline operations may not qualify merely because the airline labels them extraordinary.

Analyze:

1. What actually caused the disruption?
2. Was the event extraordinary?
3. Was it outside the carrier's actual control?
4. Could the disruption have been avoided through reasonable measures?
5. Did the carrier take all reasonable measures?

The burden of establishing the defense generally rests with the operating air carrier.

---

# 8. Compensation Calculation

Calculate separately for each passenger.

Record:

- legal basis
- distance band
- standard compensation
- applicable reduction
- individual expenses
- previous reimbursement
- amount already paid
- remaining amount claimed

Example structure:

    Passenger 1
    Article 7: €X
    Article 9: €X

    Passenger 2
    Article 7: €X
    Article 9: €X

    Total principal claim: €X

Do not combine amounts until each component has been verified.

---

# 9. Limitation & Jurisdiction

Historical claims require explicit limitation analysis.

Determine:

- relevant jurisdiction
- applicable national limitation rules
- whether a special limitation rule applies
- when the limitation period began
- whether limitation was interrupted or suspended
- whether previous correspondence affected limitation
- available court / ADR / regulator routes

Distinguish carefully between:

- limitation of a civil claim
- administrative complaint deadlines
- ADR deadlines
- Montreal Convention time limits
- EU261 Article 7 claims

Where law is uncertain, record both the supporting and opposing authorities.

Never tell a customer that a limitation position is certain when credible contrary authority exists.

---

# 10. Final Claim Assessment

Before onboarding or airline submission, prepare an internal Final Claim Assessment.

It should contain:

## Facts

What happened?

## Evidence

What can be proven?

## Itinerary

What was the contracted journey?

## Legal Basis

Why does the applicable passenger-rights regime apply?

## Liability

Why is the airline potentially responsible?

## Compensation

What should each passenger receive?

## Expenses

Which additional costs should be pursued?

## Limitation

Is the claim still actionable?

## Risks

What could cause rejection?

## Recommendation

One of:

- PURSUE
- PURSUE WITH RESERVATION
- REQUEST MORE EVIDENCE
- DO NOT PURSUE

The customer-facing assessment should explain the conclusion clearly without unnecessary legal complexity.

---

# 11. Customer Authority

Before FlightClaimly formally pursues the claim, obtain appropriate authority from every required adult passenger.

Current production architecture supports:

- primary passenger authority
- individual additional-adult authority
- secure signing links
- authority preview
- electronic signature
- individual PDFs
- shared parent claim
- admin visibility

Customers must be able to review the authority before signing.

The standard authority may permit FlightClaimly to take the claim through later stages.

However, additional litigation fees or costs require separate customer approval.

A court or local lawyer may still require a separate procedural power of attorney.

---

# 12. Manual / Legacy Claim Onboarding

Use this workflow for cases that should not pass through the normal automated customer precheck.

Examples:

- historical claims
- manually investigated claims
- claims received by email
- claims imported from another source
- flight records unavailable through normal FlightAware lookup
- exceptional journeys requiring human analysis

Target architecture:

    Internal claim review
    ↓
    Manual claim creation
    ↓
    Customer data stored
    ↓
    Secure authority link
    ↓
    Customer reviews authority
    ↓
    Customer signs
    ↓
    Additional adults sign individually
    ↓
    PDFs stored under parent claim
    ↓
    Ready for airline submission

Do not redesign the normal claim flow to accommodate exceptional historical cases.

Reuse existing claim and passenger-authority architecture wherever possible.

---

# 13. Airline Demand

The airline demand should be based on the Final Claim Assessment.

It should normally contain:

- passenger identities
- booking reference
- complete itinerary
- relevant flight numbers
- travel date
- disruption chronology
- applicable legal framework
- compensation demanded
- expenses demanded separately
- supporting evidence
- signed authority
- payment instructions where appropriate
- requested response period

The demand should be concise enough to process but sufficiently detailed to establish the claim.

Avoid generic legal threats.

Use precise law and evidence.

---

# 14. Airline Response Analysis

Do not accept an airline rejection at face value.

Classify the response.

Examples:

- extraordinary circumstances
- limitation
- no-show allegation
- late check-in
- wrong operating carrier
- compensation already paid
- insufficient documentation
- Article 7 reduction
- distance dispute
- outside EU261 scope
- duplicate claim
- authority rejected
- no substantive reason

Compare the rejection with:

- evidence
- applicable regulation
- CJEU case law
- national law
- airline's own factual assertions

Determine whether rebuttal is justified.

---

# 15. Escalation

Escalation is not automatic.

Potential routes:

- further airline correspondence
- ADR
- national enforcement body
- consumer authority
- lawyer
- court proceedings

Before recommending litigation assess:

- claim value
- legal strength
- evidentiary strength
- limitation
- jurisdiction
- procedural requirements
- likely costs
- recoverability of costs
- enforcement risk
- commercial proportionality

Any additional customer costs require explicit approval before proceeding.

---

# 16. Recovery & Payout

When compensation is recovered:

Verify:

- amount received
- passengers covered
- heads of claim covered
- FlightClaimly fee
- VAT treatment
- customer amount payable
- payment details
- payment completion

Update claim status and preserve settlement evidence.

---

# 17. Claims Intelligence

Closed claims should improve future operations.

Potential structured learnings:

- airline response time
- rejection reason
- successful rebuttal
- extraordinary circumstance category
- compensation amount
- expense recovery
- ADR outcome
- litigation outcome
- limitation result
- procedural requirement
- airline-specific behavior

Customer-specific information must remain private.

Only anonymized, validated and reusable findings may later feed the Knowledge Engine.

# Claims Desk Research Standard

For legal or regulatory conclusions, prioritize:

1. legislation / treaty text
2. CJEU or competent court decisions
3. official regulator / government guidance
4. ADR decisions or official consumer bodies
5. high-quality secondary legal analysis
6. airline statements
7. general web sources

Do not rely on SEO articles where authoritative sources are available.

When authority conflicts:

- identify the conflict
- determine hierarchy
- determine factual similarity
- record uncertainty
- avoid overstating the conclusion

---

# Claims Desk Communication Standard

## Customer Communication

Should be:

- clear
- calm
- transparent
- confident where evidence supports confidence
- explicit about material uncertainty
- free of unnecessary legal jargon

The objective is for the customer to understand:

- what FlightClaimly found
- what FlightClaimly believes
- what FlightClaimly will claim
- what the risks are
- what happens next

## Airline Communication

Should be:

- factual
- structured
- evidence-based
- legally precise
- concise
- professional

The objective is recovery, not argument for argument's sake.
