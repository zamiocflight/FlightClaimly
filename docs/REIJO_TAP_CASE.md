# CJEU REASONABLE-MEASURES / REROUTING AUTHORITY

Important authority previously identified:

CJEU Case C-74/19

This supports the proposition that an operating air carrier facing extraordinary circumstances cannot necessarily satisfy its reasonable-measures obligation merely by placing passengers on its next convenient service.

Depending on circumstances and availability, reasonable measures can require consideration of alternative direct or indirect flights, potentially including flights operated by other carriers, unless doing so would impose an intolerable sacrifice on the carrier.

Use this authority carefully.

For this case the particularly powerful factual point is even simpler:

TAP itself operated an earlier LIS → FAO service, TP1909, on the same day.

Again:

Do NOT claim we have proved seats were available.

Ask TAP to demonstrate why that earlier service could not be used.

# LIMITATION / PRESCRIPTION ISSUE

This is a 2020 flight being pursued in 2026.

This issue has already been investigated.

Reijo reported that a senior specialist at the Finnish European Consumer Centre / consumer-rights side advised him that claims in Portugal can have a limitation period of up to:

20 years.

Important CJEU authority:

C-139/11
Cuadrench Moré

Core point:

The limitation period for claims for standardized compensation under Regulation 261/2004 is governed by the national law applicable to the action.

It is NOT automatically subject to the Montreal Convention's two-year limitation period.

Portuguese Civil Code Article 309 provides an ordinary limitation period of:

20 years.

This gives a serious basis for arguing that the claim is not time-barred.

IMPORTANT QUALIFICATION:

Do NOT promise the customer that the Portuguese 20-year period unquestionably governs every procedural route or that litigation success on limitation is guaranteed.

Portuguese procedural/case-law application can require deeper assessment if the matter reaches litigation.

But TAP should NOT simply dismiss the Regulation 261 compensation claim by asserting a generic one-year or two-year aviation limitation without addressing the applicable national limitation law and CJEU C-139/11.

Also distinguish:

ANAC administrative complaint deadlines

from:

civil claim limitation.

An expired administrative complaint window is not automatically the same thing as extinction of the underlying civil claim.
